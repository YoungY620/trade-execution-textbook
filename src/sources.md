# References and source material

## 1. What this page is

This page lists every source this book is built on: the papers, books, lecture notes and
official documents that were actually consulted while the chapters were written, grouped by
school or theme and sorted by year within each group. It closes with what could not be
obtained, why, and where a reader can lawfully get it.

**What "consulted" means here.** An item is admitted only when the rights holder or an
authorised channel published it: arXiv, an open conference or journal version, an
institutional repository, the author's own page or self-archived preprint, a publisher-
authorised free edition or preview, or an official document issued by a regulator, exchange
or protocol. Pirate mirrors (Sci-Hub, LibGen, Anna's Archive, "free ebook download"
aggregators, forum attachments) were not used anywhere. Every catalogue entry below was read
in full as a PDF, or the item *is itself* the primary source (an official release, a live fee
schedule, a publisher's own front matter). No entry is a paraphrase of an abstract, with four
explicit exceptions that are flagged on the entry itself and listed again in Section 3:
Amihud–Mendelson (1986), O'Hara–Ye (2011), Kolm–Ritter (2019) and Aleti–Mizrach (2021).

**Status markers.** The same four markers are used in the English and Chinese editions:

- **已获全文** — full text obtained and read (open PDF, author copy, repository copy, or a
  complete author/publisher-issued draft).
- **公开讲义或预览（书）** — for a commercial book: the author's or publisher's own lecture
  material, teaching draft, or official preview/front matter was obtained, not the printed body.
- **官方文件** — primary documentary evidence (a regulator's release, an exchange/protocol
  document, a live fee schedule) rather than a peer-reviewed paper.
- **未获取全文** — no lawful full text obtained; the entry is cited from secondary or abstract-
  level material and is listed in Section 3.

### 4.2 The "over 60% potential savings" claim

A claim in the draft chapters says that optimising against the **complete fee schedule** yields
**more than 60% potential savings**. A full-corpus search — every obtained PDF converted to text
and searched for `60%`, `savings`, `% saving`, `cost reduction` and route-improvement phrasings —
**did not verify this figure.** No passage in any source reports a >60% cost saving from
fee-schedule optimisation. The `60%` matches found are unrelated (e.g. a common-component
volume share, a sample-coverage share, an annualised basis deviation, a noise-trading share).
The papers that do model a full fee schedule (He et al.; Lehar et al.; the Binance fee schedule)
report Sharpe ratios, no-arbitrage bands and liquidity shares, not percentage cost savings. The
nearest measured "savings" numbers are far below 60%: Xi & Moallemi find realised DEX routing
loses about **2.02 bps per trade** (≈$24M over ≈$120B) versus optimal, and Diamandis et al.'s
">50%" figure is the solver's objective value against a baseline, not a trading-cost saving.
**The 60% figure should be treated as unverified** and the textbook should not repeat it without
an explicit, verifiable citation.
